History

Twenty‑five years of reading meters

QtDMM began as a one‑person project for a Metex meter on a Linux box and has been passed from maintainer to maintainer ever since.

  • 2001

    First release by Matthias Toussaint

    A Qt 2/3 application for Metex and Voltcraft meters with a serial cable: digital readout, a simple analog dial and a hand‑painted transient recorder. Licensed under the GPL v2.

  • 2005 – 2009

    0.8.x — the community list grows

    Users send in protocol captures and QtDMM learns PeakTech, Mastech, McVoice, Radioshack, Digitech and more (see the AUTHORS file). Threshold‑triggered recording, integration curve, external command execution and printing arrive.

  • 2014

    0.9.0 — Qt 4 and GPL v3

    Port to Qt 4, new build system, licence changed to GPL v3.

  • 2015 – 2016

    0.9.5 – 0.9.7 — tuxmaster takes over

    Tuxmaster ports QtDMM to Qt 5, fixes translations, logging and CSV export, and becomes the upstream maintainer. Voltcraft VC870 support with apparent‑power and power‑factor symbols.

  • 2025 – 2026

    1.0 — Qt 6, CMake, new instruments

    Complete move to Qt 6 and C++17 with a CMake build; macOS, Windows (installer, USB‑HID) and FreeBSD in CI. New connection backends: USB‑HID cables, RFC2217 remote serial, sigrok. Decoders for Uni‑T UT61E, UT70B, UT71x, UT803/804, PeakTech 3315, DTM0660‑based meters — each verified against recorded protocol logs. Recorder rebuilt on Qt Charts. New analog needle instrument and LCD display panels. Calculated values across instances, a built‑in virtual meter, qtdmm‑bridge to serve meters from a Raspberry Pi, and Bluetooth LE for Victron battery monitors, solar chargers and inverters; an SCPI server that makes any of them a network instrument. Handbook embedded in the program and published as this site's documentation; interface in five languages.
