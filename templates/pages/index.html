Your multimeter, on screen.

Features

Everything a bench readout should do

Made for hobbyists, repair shops and labs who want to see and log what their meter measures — without proprietary software. The full handbook is built in (F1) and online.

◔

Analog needle instrument

Studio VU‑meter style dial with needle ballistics, red overrange zone and OL lamp. Auto‑ranging scale, dark or ivory face.

▤

LCD display panel

Seven‑segment digits, bargraph, min/max, mode annunciators — scales with the window and docks anywhere.

∿

Transient recorder

Start manually, at a scheduled time or when a threshold is crossed. Zoom, pan, cursor read‑out, integration curve, print and CSV export. Alarms raise a banner, beep, popup or program when the reading leaves its range, overloads or stops coming.

▦

Readings table

Every reading as a row — time, value, unit, mode, range — with live statistics, copy to spreadsheet and CSV export. The raw protocol next to the recorder's curve.

⇄

Any cable, any port

RS‑232 and USB‑serial, USB‑HID cables (Uni‑Trend UT‑D04/D09, Brymen BU‑86X), RFC2217 over the network — with qtdmm‑bridge on a Raspberry Pi — Bluetooth LE, or sigrok‑cli for SCPI bench meters (Keysight, Agilent, HP, Siglent).

⧉

Several meters, calculated values

One QtDMM per meter, all recording in sync — and a third instance that calculates from them: P = U·I, a voltage drop, a probe factor. A built‑in virtual meter (constant, sine, noise, discharge curve …) stands in for hardware you don't have or lets you test without any.

Bluetooth LE · Victron

Your battery, on the bench

QtDMM listens to the encrypted Bluetooth advertisements of Victron's SmartShunt / BMV‑712 battery monitors, SmartSolar / BlueSolar MPPT chargers and the Phoenix Inverter Smart — no connection, no pairing, no root. The device key comes from the VictronConnect app; a Scan button in the settings lists what is in range.

  • Pick the value that becomes the reading — display, needle, recorder — and a second one for the display's lower line and the readings table
  • SmartShunt: battery voltage, current, power, state of charge, consumed Ah, time to go, aux input
  • MPPT: PV power, battery voltage, charging current, yield today, load current · Phoenix: AC power, voltage, current
  • Qt 6 Bluetooth, Linux via BlueZ; verified live on all three device types

qtdmm‑bridge

Meters in the lab, QtDMM on your desk

A Raspberry Pi or any old box next to the bench runs qtdmm‑bridge — one Python file, pyserial its only dependency — and serves each meter on a TCP port. QtDMM connects over RFC 2217 and sends baud rate, parity and DTR/RTS itself, so the bridge knows nothing about meters: every model QtDMM supports works through it unchanged.

  • One TCP port per meter, stable by‑id device names, reconnect after re‑plugging — and QtDMM reconnects itself after a bridge restart
  • Serial cables and USB‑HID cables (UT‑D04, UT‑D09, Brymen BU‑86X) alike — HID meters remote for the first time
  • Command line, or a TOML config that --print-config writes from what it finds; systemd unit; announces itself by mDNS — Find bridges in QtDMM lists every bridge on the network
  • No authentication — keep it on a trusted LAN or behind an SSH tunnel
  • Running an early copy? Update to qtdmm_bridge.py 0.2.0 — it fixes swapped odd/even parity, which broke 7O1 meters such as the UT61E over the bridge

                    --port 4001=hid:1a86:e008

# in QtDMM: Settings → Special ports
#   type RFC2217, address raspberry:4000
#   then pick the meter model as usual
